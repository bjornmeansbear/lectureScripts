# Proposal: Everything is a Motion Graphic

Hayelin Choi & Kristian Bjornard. Written October 2022 for the MODE Summit call for proposals (<https://www.modesummit.com/call-for-proposals>). Recovered from Google Drive 2026-10-08. Whether it was submitted or accepted is not recorded here.

Format: Long Paper

## Title

Everything is a Motion Graphic: Expanding the definition of graphic design in motion.

## Abstract

Looking up various definitions of “Motion Graphics” one learns that the discipline is seen as basically “animated graphic design.” But what does this mean? As screens have permeated every surface of our lives, it is easy to think of “motion graphics” or “animated graphic design” increasingly in terms of just that — graphic design moving on screens, designed animations, or cleverly crafted additions to existing video content.

However, time, motion, and change are acting on everything around us, and all of our perceptions, whether we intend it to or not. Things exist, time passes. Posters get hung, people walk past them. We take a bike ride and read signs and billboards as we travel alongside. The livery of an airplane brand zooms above us. When we visit a supermarket or a mall with music in the background, we amble around dimensional and typographic sculpture. As we are experiencing the change, our eyes function like a film camera. Our eyes pan, focus, and pause to read a headline, a stop sign, and a bag of chips. We see an advertisement from our childhood years later and still feel moved. Motion graphics are happening around us all the time, but our traditional definition of motion graphics is inadequate to explore this new perspective within the realms of education.

These other design scenarios are further chances to intentionally think about “motion graphics” and how students encounter it in academia. Through a variety of examples we will present motion design definitions that allow concepts of change over time to encompass not only animated designs on screens, but also design as it exists out in the real world, traveling and changing through time.

The end goal is to see *everything* as a motion graphic, which can lead to the start of a different pedagogical approach to motion graphics. This challenges educators to focus more on *time + motion + change* in graphic design — not necessarily on software and technology. To facilitate a vision of motion graphics as foundational in design curriculum, we must redesign motion graphics education to be more accessible for both students and design faculty from the very beginning.

## Broad outline

- Provoke with the initial starting question: How do you know something is a motion graphic?
- Provide quick, basic working definitions of motion graphics from the field,
- Explain through historical and contemporary examples how this definition is limited
- Provide new definition that encompasses other ways “motion” or “change” might still live in non-traditional motion design works
- Deliver examples that fit into new definition
- Lay out how *time + motion + change* can become more foundational design curriculum concepts.
- Persuade the audience that everything is a motion graphic.

## Keywords

Perception, continuity, sequence, movement, Change
