The power problem

You came in through management. You read as management-adjacent, whatever you say.

“No” is free for you and expensive for them.

  • —Never approach anyone mid-task.
  • —One “no” ends it. No rephrasing, no trying their coworker instead.
Memorize this. Three sentences.
Hi — I’m a design student at MICA. We’re doing a class project about how this building works, and I’d love to hear how your job actually goes if you have a few minutes.

Nothing you say goes to management with your name on it, and you can stop any time or tell me to go away.

Is now okay, or is there a better time?

Who you are and what you want · what happens to their words and that they can stop · control of the timing. If you can’t say it without reading it, you’re not ready to talk to anybody.

ethnos + graphein

A people, and writing. Ethnography literally means writing people down.

The output of the method is a written account. Not a photo, not a video, not a feeling you had in a lobby. That’s why we spend the back half of this afternoon on how to write.

Malinowski · Argonauts of the Western Pacific · 1922

Stay long enough that people forget to perform for you.

Stop reading other people’s reports. Go live there. Learn the language. He was there for years.

The American line
1858–1942Franz Boas, Columbia. Cultural relativism — you understand a practice in its own terms, not by ranking it against your own. Design ethnography runs entirely on this idea and almost never credits it.
1891–1960Zora Neale Hurston studied under Boas at Barnard. Mules and Men, 1935 — folklore collected in Florida and Louisiana, in her own community. She was an insider, and she wrote about what that changed.

Boas trained Margaret Mead and Ruth Benedict too. Most of what you think of as anthropology comes out of one classroom.

Where the method comes from, honestly

Anthropology grew up in the same decades as European empire.

A great deal of early fieldwork happened in colonized territories, sometimes with colonial administrations as patrons or as the audience for the report. Studying people who cannot refuse to be studied was, for a long time, the normal condition of the discipline.

It has spent a century reckoning with that. You’re inheriting the method and the reckoning at the same time.

Be honest about the theft

An anthropologist spends eighteen months. You get five days across a semester.

Design took anthropology’s methods and left behind the timescale, the language learning, the reflexivity, and most of the ethics apparatus.

Knowing you’re doing a compressed, partial version makes you careful. Believing you’ve “done ethnography” because you filled a wall with Post-its makes you dangerous.

1986 — Writing Culture, eds. James Clifford and George Marcus

You are in the account whether you admit it or not.

The reflexive turn. Ethnographic writing is constructed, partial, and rhetorical — somebody chose what to include, in what order, in whose words.

You are not a camera. Write down who you were in the room.

How it got into design
1984Lucy Suchman finishes a PhD in social and cultural anthropology at Berkeley and is working at Xerox PARC.
1987Plans and Situated Actions, Cambridge University Press. She videotaped people trying and failing to use a photocopier. Generally called the first corporate ethnography.
1987Her argument: people don’t follow plans. They improvise from whatever is in front of them, moment to moment. The machine assumed otherwise, so it was unusable.

Jottings

Fieldnotes

Afterward. Expanding the jottings while you can still see it in your head.

Full fieldnotes within twenty-four hours.

Memory for this collapses overnight. You keep the gist and lose every specific, and the specifics are the entire value. We write notes on the light rail home.

“Yeah the mornings are the worst, nobody tells us anything”
Commentary Is the sign the problem, or is the desk in the wrong place? Ask: does this happen all day?

“The worst” — worst how? Volume? Communication? FOLLOW UP.

What a camera would have caught, on the left. What you think it means, on the right.

Some guidelines

  • 01Don't Mix Your Thoughts Don't let interpretation get written as observation.
  • 02Verbatim quotes get quotation marks. Everything else is paraphrase. A fake quote is worse than no quote.
  • 03Time-stamp constantly. “Waited 6 seconds” is worth more than “waited a while,” and it is nearly impossible to reconstruct it later.

Also: draw. Record what’s absent — the chair nobody sits in. Write down what surprised you, and that it surprised you.

Coding · Glaser & Strauss, 1967
  • 01Open coding. Tag each observation with a short label. No categories decided in advance.
  • 02In vivo codes. Use the person’s own words. “the mornings are the worst” — not “morning workflow inefficiency.”
  • 03Axial coding. Group them. What clusters? That’s the affinity wall.
The rule for the wall

One observation per note.
No interpretation on the note itself.

If the interpretation is already written on the card, you’ve pre-decided the answer and the wall is theater.

That’s Jen’s Post-it critique, answered. Same Post-its. The difference is entirely in what’s written on them.
