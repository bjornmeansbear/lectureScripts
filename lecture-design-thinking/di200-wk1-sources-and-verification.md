## 1 · Verified this session

### Christopher Alexander's repudiation — CONFIRMED

> "I reject the whole idea of design methods as a subject of study, since I think it is
> absurd to separate the study of designing from the practice of design."

**Source:** new preface to the 1971 paperback edition of *Notes on the Synthesis of Form*
(originally Harvard University Press, 1964).

Context confirmed: by 1971 an academic field had grown up around the book, and Alexander
was being described as a leading figure in it. The preface repudiates the formal method
the 1964 book had laid out.

Corroborating: <https://www.buildingsandcities.org/insights/commentaries/christopher-alexander-synthesis-form.html>

This is the strongest quote in the whole history section. The author of the founding text
disowning the field, inside the preface of that same text.

### John Christopher Jones — biography CONFIRMED

Via <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Chris_Jones>

- Full name John Christopher Jones. Welsh. 7 October 1927 – 13 August 2022.
- Worked for Associated Electrical Industries (AEI), Manchester.
- One of the instigators of the 1962 Conference on Design Methods, which led to the
  formation of the **Design Research Society**. Chaired the DRS 1971–1973.
- First **Professor of Design at the Open University**, 1970–1974.
- *Design Methods: Seeds of Human Futures*, Wiley, 1970.
- Later worked as an independent designer, researcher, and writer. *Designing Designing*, 1991.

### Horst Rittel at Ulm — CONFIRMED, and this is the good one

- **Professor of Design Methodology at the Hochschule für Gestaltung Ulm, 1958–1963.**
  Taught operations research and cybernetics there.
- Recruited to **UC Berkeley in 1963** by William Wurster, Dean of the College of
  Environmental Design.
- Founded the **Design Methods Group** at Berkeley in **1967**.
- With Melvin Webber, "Dilemmas in a General Theory of Planning," *Policy Sciences* 4, 1973.

Sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_Rittel> ·
<https://www.dubberly.com/articles/why-horst-wj-rittel-matters.html>

**Why this matters for the lecture:** the job title. "Professor of Design Methodology,"
1958, at a Bauhaus-descended school — five decades before anyone sold a workshop in it.
And the same person produces the field's most durable idea fifteen years later. The
Bauhaus → Ulm → Rittel → Berkeley → wicked problems chain is documentary, not analogy.

### Lucy Suchman — CONFIRMED

- PhD in **social and cultural anthropology, UC Berkeley, 1984.** Worked at Xerox PARC.
- *Plans and Situated Actions: The Problem of Human-Machine Communication*, Cambridge
  University Press, **1987**.
- Her ethnographic study — video of office workers struggling to use a photocopier — is
  "today often referred to as one of the first instances of corporate ethnography."
- Argument: people do not work from plans in the sense of stepwise abstract actions; they
  work from **situated actions** — local, sensed alternatives at each moment. This is the
  book that broke the information-processing model's hold on HCI.
- Source: <https://womenofixd.com/stories/lucy-suchman> · CV:
  <https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/doc_library/sociology/suchman_cv>

**Why it matters for the lecture:** this is the ethnography-side equivalent of the Rittel
find. A documented path from academic anthropology into corporate design practice, through
one named person, with a dated book.

**Not verified:** the identities of the two people in the famous copier video. They are
often named as eminent computer scientists. Don't name them from the podium.

### Bauhaus Vorkurs — CONFIRMED

Via the Bauhaus-Archiv and bauhaus imaginista:
<https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/article/preliminary-course-workshops-and-bauhaus-diploma/> ·
<https://www.bauhaus-imaginista.org/articles/5176/three-preliminary-courses-itten-moholy-nagy-albers/>

- The preliminary course ran the full life of the school, 1919–1933, taught by only three
  people in sequence: **Johannes Itten**, then **László Moholy-Nagy**, then **Josef Albers**.
- Stated purpose: teach elementary design categories — dimension and proportion, statics
  and dynamics, tension and contrast — "by means of their own experimental activities
  (the 'learning by doing' principle)," plus a working understanding of how different
  materials behave.
- Activities: paper folding, collage, model building.
- The three differed: Itten intuitive and spiritual, using waste and transient materials;
  Moholy-Nagy rationalist, focused on light and new materials; Albers sober and industrial.
  Under Albers and Moholy-Nagy material was approached "with a pragmatic curiosity similar
  to a scientific experiment."

### IDEO's contraction — CONFIRMED

Via *Fast Company*, November 2023:
<https://www.fastcompany.com/90976682/design-giant-ideo-cuts-a-third-of-staff-and-closes-offices-as-the-era-of-design-thinking-ends>

- **October 2023:** CEO Derek Robson announced cutting **125 jobs, ~25% of the global
  workforce.**
- Including earlier 2023 cuts, **about one third of all staff** that year.
- Closed **Munich and Tokyo** offices; reduced footprint in London, Shanghai, and three
  US locations.
- **Roughly half the headcount gone since 2020.**
- A former employee is quoted saying revenue went from ~$300M/year to ~$100M "in just a
  few years." *Treat that number as a single unnamed source, not as reported fact.*
- Fast Company's own framing was that the design thinking era was ending.
- Follow-on, not used on any slide: IDEO named Mike Peng CEO in 2025, per *Fortune*.

### The two Week-2 readings — CONFIRMED, both free

- **Andrew Blauvelt, "Design's Ethnographic Turn,"** Design Observer, **1 May 2007**.
  Full text, no paywall: <https://designobserver.com/designs-ethnographic-turn/>
  Confirmed content: opens with him receiving an AIGA ethnography primer; argues this is a
  "third phase of modern design" concerned with design's effects beyond the object; the

  **Full text free on the author's own site**, which is the link to give students:
  <https://www.jonkolko.com/writing/abductive-thinking-and-sensemaking>

## 3 · Could NOT verify — do not project

### The J. Christopher Jones quote

> "the machine language, the behaviourism, the continual attempt to fix the whole of life
> into a logical framework"

This is quoted **everywhere** — design blogs, lecture decks, secondary literature — as
Jones explaining why he turned against design methods in the 1970s. Searching for it
turns up the quote endlessly but never a citation to a specific text, page, or edition.
Candidate homes if you want to chase it: the preface to the second edition of *Design
Methods* (1980), *Designing Designing* (1991), or "How My Thoughts About Design Methods
Have Changed During the Years" (*Design Methods and Theories*, 1977).

**It has been removed from the deck and the script.** Alexander's 1971 line is used
instead — it makes the same point and it's sourced.

The substance of Jones's turn *is* documented and is safe to state in your own words: he
moved through the 1970s away from systematic method toward chance, openness, and
improvisation, influenced by John Cage.

### Natasha Jen's exact wording

The substance of her 99U talk is not in dispute — religion/dogma framing, the Post-it as
emblem, missing evidence and expertise, "where's the criticism?" But I have not verified
her phrasing word-for-word against the recording. The deck presents her argument as
paraphrase in bullets, with only "Where's the criticism?" set as a direct quote.

**Check that one line against the video before projecting it:**
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V8gjDsW3lsY>

## 5 · The lineage, assembled

The through-line the lecture is built on, in one place:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1452–1519 | Leonardo — drawing as thinking, models as tests, direct observation over authority |
| 1919–1933 | Bauhaus *Vorkurs* — learning by doing, as curriculum |
| 1920–1930 | Vkhutemas, Moscow — the parallel version |
| 1927 | Max Bill enrolls at the Bauhaus, Dessau |
| 1953 | Bill co-founds HfG Ulm — Bauhaus plus cybernetics, operations research, semiotics |
| **1958** | **Rittel becomes Professor of Design Methodology at Ulm** |
| 1962 | Conference on Design Methods, London (Jones) → Design Research Society, 1966 |
| 1963 | Berkeley recruits Rittel |
| 1964 | Alexander, *Notes on the Synthesis of Form* |
| 1967 | Design Methods Group founded at Berkeley |
| 1969 | Simon, *The Sciences of the Artificial* |
| 1970 | Jones, *Design Methods* |
| **1971** | **Alexander repudiates design methods in his own preface** |
| 1971 | Papanek, *Design for the Real World* |
| 1973 | Rittel & Webber — wicked problems |
| 1982–83 | Cross; Schön — the rebuild |
| **1992** | **Buchanan — "design thinking" enters the literature** |
| 1991–2009 | IDEO; The Deep Dive; d.school; Double Diamond; Brown in HBR |

Practice: about five hundred years old. Academic study: about seventy. The brand: about twenty.
