Out on campus · 60 min

One post, in pairs. You stay in it. Draw it, walk it, count it, then sit and write fifty numbered observations.

Back in this room · 15 min

Turn the jottings into fieldnotes while it is still in your head. Before lunch, not after.

You hand in:  fifty numbered lines · one plan drawing · one count with a real number · ten lines with a count or duration · five about something absent · three verbatim quotes · one thing you cannot explain.

What if there is nobody there
  • 01Empty is data. Write the time and write that it was empty. An entrance nobody uses at 11:40 on a Monday is a finding, not a wasted hour.
  • 02Read the traces instead. Wear, desire paths, propped doors, what has been moved, what is broken and taped, every hand-lettered sign.
  • 03Then turn the method on yourself. You are a person in the space. Walk it as a stranger and record your own experience with the same discipline — timestamps, where you hesitated, what you could not find.
That third one has a name · Carolyn Ellis and Arthur Bochner

Auto-ethnography.

The researcher’s own experience treated as data, and analysed the same way you would analyse anyone else’s.

You are already doing a light version of it. The service safari is auto-ethnography with a stopwatch. The rules do not change: timestamp it, say what happened before you say what it meant, and mark clearly that the person in the note is you.
