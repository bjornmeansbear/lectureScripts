# Semiotic Still Life (and Flags)

Project 1, Advanced Design 1 (GD400) · first run at MICA, Fall 2017; rerun 2018, 2019, 2023 (AD1) and in FF120 Color Design, Fall 2024 · goes with [Some Semiotics](lecture-some-semiotics.md) · CC BY-SA 4.0

About four weeks. Starts as a simple poster, with twists at the end.

## The brief

Much of graphic design is the making of meaning by manipulating signs and symbols: analogy, metaphor, symbolism, signification. What something means, what it says, how it says it. Connotation and denotation.

Use the tradition of still life to make an abstract self-portrait. Compose and photograph a still life of objects that symbolically represent your influences, inspirations and needs as a designer. (In 2018 the version for seniors was narrower: the ideas you're thinking of exploring in your degree project, in 1–15 objects.)

Each object should be chosen for a reason. It should be a sign for an idea, not just itself. In a separate document, list the objects and what each one means.

Questions to work from:

- What literal objects represent your topic?
- What metaphors apply to it?
- What allusions can you make, in design, art, architecture, literature, history?
- What personal influences and interests do you want to bring in?

Think about art direction, semiotics and metaphor as you compose the picture, not only as you choose the objects.

### Part 2A: the flag

Condense your still life down to a flag. Keep thinking about signs and what they mean. Is it one beautiful shape on a field of color? What other imagery, or what simplification, carries the same messages and feelings as the still life and still works as a flag?

### Part 2B: the instructions

“Good graphic design is something you can explain to someone over the telephone.” (Bob Gill)

Write clear instructions for making your flag. In class, make each other's flags from the instructions alone. Do at least four.

## Deliverables

1. Photographic print (2017: 10×16 in; 2018: 11×8.5 in landscape, printed and trimmed with bleed)
2. List of objects and their meaning, typeset from the template
3. Digital version as JPG or PDF
4. Your flag
5. Instructions for making it
6. The flags other people made from your instructions

## Schedule (4–5 weeks)

1. Project assigned. Brainstorm your list of objects and what they mean.
2. Present objects (real things or photos) and rough compositions.
3. Draft composition for critique. Object list due.
4. Final print, installed as a class show. Then flags and instructions in small groups.
5. Final files and documentation.

## Notes for reuse

- The flag step is where the semiotics gets tested: the still life can lean on context and many objects, but the flag has to carry the same meaning in a few shapes and colors. The telephone instructions then test whether the form can be described at all.
- The same still-life-to-flag sequence ran in FF120 Color Design (Fall 2024) as A4: Still Life + Flags. The color version is a good one for first-years.
- Companion pieces: the [semiotic commons workshop](../workshop-semiotic-commons/workshop-semiotic-commons.md) uses the same signs-and-meaning ground with zines and posters. DI220's P4, Semiotic Signage System (Spring 2027), is in the private `syllabiBuilder` until that term ends.
