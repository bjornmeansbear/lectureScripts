# Subjective Dictionary

Project 3, Advanced Design 1 (GD400) · first run at MICA, Fall 2017; rerun Fall 2018 · goes with [Some Semiotics](lecture-some-semiotics.md) · CC BY-SA 4.0

Five to six weeks in 2017; three weeks in 2018.

## Background

When you think of a dictionary, you probably think of a linear, passive, neutral index of words and meanings. Alphabetical order gives you a way to navigate that objective system. Gustave Flaubert's *Dictionary of Accepted Ideas* (compiled over decades, published after his death; English translation 1954) is a subjective dictionary. It borrows the authority of the form and fills it with personal feelings, ideas and wit.

## The brief

Make a dictionary for your degree project (or any long project you're about to start). Research and define the terms, ideas and visual reference points that belong to the topic. You may have an idea of the topic already while the final product is still unfocused. That's OK. This assignment fills conceptual gaps, refines the direction, and gives you something to move forward on with confidence.

Organize the visual and verbal vocabulary of the project into a form that clarifies by example, and that may raise new questions by how it's designed. It can take any shape (a book, a linked website, a deck of cards) as long as a dictionary is its starting point. Use it to try ideas, find content, and understand the topic.

## Things to consider

- What do you want this document to say about your project, through design, narrative and navigation?
- Include things that explain what your project is, and also what it is not.
- Is every letter used equally, or do you emphasize, amplify, omit?
- What other ways can you set up hierarchies or relationships between entries?
- Can the document be exploration (growing, changing ideas) and classification at the same time?
- Can images be freed from a subordinate role?
- Can words be freed from their expected meanings?

## Deliverables

1. A dictionary with at least one term for each letter, A to Z. That's at least 26 entries. More is fine, and you can skip letters or use numbers if you still reach 26 (or have a good reason for leaving some out).
2. A project statement of about 100 words (“my degree project will be X”).
3. A bibliography of at least 10 sources.
4. Photo or video documentation, plus PDF or digital source files.

## Schedule

1. Introduction. Read Rick Poynor, “Love of Lexicons.” Library day. Pick the topic you hope your degree project will be.
2. Idea pitches: three possible forms, word lists (at least 26 terms; if you're stuck, find one thing per letter), and the start of at least 10 outside sources (books, sites, image archives, journal articles, news stories, blog posts, other design projects on similar subjects).
3. Draft of the 100-word topic description. Work in progress.
4. Work in progress.
5. Dictionary due. Critique.

## Notes for reuse

- The semiotics connection: a dictionary is a list of signs and what they mean, and a subjective one admits that the meanings are chosen. Students end up writing their own codebook.
- It works outside a degree-project year. Any long project, a thesis, or a studio's research phase can start here.
