# Typologies

Project 2, Advanced Design 1 (GD400) · first run at MICA, Fall 2017 · goes with [Some Semiotics](lecture-some-semiotics.md) · CC BY-SA 4.0

Six to seven weeks.

*Typology: a systematic classification or study of types, usually by characteristics or traits in common.*

## The brief

Look again at everyday objects and experiences. Visual research and intuitive making lead to experiments, and the experiments lead to posters.

Start with a chapter of Jessica Helfand's *Design: The Invention of Desire* (2016). Each chapter is an essay on one topic: Authority, Fantasy, Identity, Consequence, Compassion, Patience, Solitude, Melancholy, Humility, Memory, Desire, Change. Pair it with a knowledge domain of your own, ideally a possible degree-project topic. The outcomes are better when the two don't already line up.

Research online and in the library (the library is required). Free-associate and stray from the original subject as you see fit. The goal is to reconsider your relationship to something that once seemed familiar, and to see what it means for design and culture at large.

## Parts

**Part 1: typology study (1 week).**
- At least 10 words that describe your Helfand chapter, and at least 10 for your own domain.
- At least three aphorisms or quotes for each.
- Images, illustrations, diagrams and patterns from digital and analog sources. What literal objects represent the topic? What metaphors apply? What allusions can you make, in design, art, architecture, literature, history? What personal interests do you want to bring in?
- Present as a PDF slideshow. Last page is a bibliography.

**Part 2: 20 experimental compositions (1 week).** Ten 8×8 in collages for the Helfand chapter, ten for your own topic, printed and trimmed. Explore what the words and images can become when you manipulate type and image, analog and digital. How does a word's meaning change in a different typeface? How do type and image make space: shadow, plane, perspective, overlay, depth? Go wild. Abstract, make modular, texture, pattern, cut, tear, float, fold, frame, mask, project, adorn, alter.

**Part 3: connections (1 week).** A map of the connections between the Part 2 compositions, plus five more experiments in whatever format the connections suggest. Look for coincidences, visual and verbal. Could one composition be the grid for a poster? Could two combine? Did a technique turn up that you want to carry forward?

**Part 4: two “posters” (2 weeks).** Two posters that start a dialogue about your topic and push the audience to think critically. They can express complementary or contrasting points of view. 18×24 in or the pixel equivalent. Each one uses a grid, has three levels of hierarchy including a heading, at least 200 words of text, and at least one image. Interpret “poster” as your content needs: a video still, a website, an app, signage.

**Documentation (after critique).** Archive everything: research, process documents, sketches, writing, experiments, failures, finals. “I want everything you made during this whole project!”
