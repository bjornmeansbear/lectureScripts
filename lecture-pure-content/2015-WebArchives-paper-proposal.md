## Title

Towards Purer Content.

## Abstract

The end-form of the content should dictate the style — visual style should not be baked into the content itself. The content should remain structured, yet style-less (WYSIWYM over WYSIWYG [What-you-see-is-what-you-mean instead of what-you-see-is-what-you-get]). Structure must be semantically implied with simple meta data integrated into content. This structured, un-styled, meta data rich content can then be fed to whatever service, tool, program, etc. required for any type of desired display.

We are not yet in this perfect world.

The common tools we use are ill-suited for un-styled content creation. Word processors wrap text in formatting hard to remove for use in other contexts. Desktop publishing platforms lock content into layout files incompatible with other workflows. WYSIWYG tools bloat content with impure markup saved in relational databases making content there hard to reuse or migrate too. This impure content withers and dies, chained into a file format, storage method, or self-imposed style prison.

This does not have to be the case.

Purer content is achievable. Pure content is able to evolve and live in different templates, different places, and migrate seamlessly between different future-friendly formats.

Several experiments will showcase a variety of ways that content can better conform to an idealized "pure" form that is more flexible and more future friendly than current options.

## Bio, as submitted

Kristian Bjornard
Faculty, Graphic Design, Maryland Institute College of Art / Director, The Office of Kristian Bjornard

Kristian Bjørnard is a designer, educator, and sustainabilitist; and was the 2013/2014 Bunting Teaching Fellow in Graphic Design at the Maryland Institute College of Art (MICA). He is the director of "The Office of Kristian Bjørnard," a design studio focused on publishing in all its myriad forms.

Kristian currently teaches courses in web design, motion graphics, information visualization, and sustainability at MICA. Current research includes sustainability in a graphic design context, emerging web technologies, and new print and digital publishing tools. This has resulted in various "sustainable" aesthetic exercises, a more purposeful interest into systems, exploring reusable processes, interest in rules-based design concepts, and investigating vernacular design methodologies. Professional work revolves around traditional publishing, dynamic identities, materials for a Baltimore Museum, streamlining project processes, and attempts at digital products.

Kristian holds an MFA in Graphic Design from the Maryland Institute College of Art and a BA in Studio Art from Kalamazoo College.
