## The Workshop Portion

Workshop (4:15-6:30/7:00pm)

4:15 - 4:30pm: Break
4:30 - 6:30/7:00pm: Workshop with GD juniors (R3113)

After the “Design the Future Today” lecture, we shall generate some of our own futures, images, and objects.

### 1. Remind students of key points from the lecture (4:30pm)

The preferable future is unique to each individual.
Ask the question: If something doesn't fit in my preferable future, should I make it at all in the present?

How a text-to-image diffusion model works.
smart prompt writing.
AI ethics and other considerations…

Max 20min

Kristian briefly talks in front of class again. Goes over Figma doc quickly too.

### 2. Write down your ideas for a preferable future (4:50pm)

Be descriptive in a paragraph or less.

If you can’t envision something broad or comprehendible in this time, can you at least think about your longer term self’s future — what kind of world do you personally want to live in, what kind of work do you want to be able to do, etc.

≈10 Minutes

“Alone together” exercise — we’re all on our computers/laptops in figma doing this. Kristian floats around to answer questions or help.

—

If you need help: see the Stuart Candy method for seeing different KINDS of possibles
“Four Generic Futures”
Continuation (further status quo growth and change, eg., ‘business as usual’)
Limits and Discipline (behaviors to adapt to growing internal or environmental limits)
Decline and Collapse (system degradation or failure modes as crises emerge)
Transformation (new technology, business, or social factors that disruptively change the system)

If you need help you can also checkout Project Drawdown — a list of the 100 most effective tools for reducing C02 in the atmosphere.

### 3. Turn your preferable future descriptions into prompts for text-to-image tools (5:00pm)

Try your prompts multiple times and experiment with tweaking them for better results
What ways can you turn your description into prompts?
try just inputting the whole description
try pulling out some keywords
try rewriting bits and pieces to make simpler “chunks” of descriptions
Save your outputs (at least 4!). 
The “Collections of Student Work” board has templates for you to use for this. Track the tool and prompt for each image
be doing this by 5:15pm
Select your favorite image and re-write a description of that visualized future, along with the prompt and tool that generated the image 
be doing this by 5:20pm.

Total of 20–30 Minutes

“Alone together” exercise — we’re all on our computers/laptops in figma doing this. Kristian floats around to answer questions or help.

Students should be able to duplicate figma “components” to easily fill in and capture their content.

### 4. Quickly Share and discuss the prompt results (5:30pm)

Analyze the reasons if the results were not usable.
Brainstorm ways to improve the prompt and the tools' responses.

≈15 Minutes

Project in front of class, students still can look around on their own in Figma. We’ll try to collect the group’s answers to what was working/what wasn’t and how to improve.

Comment/edit in and on figma “pages”

### 5. Reflect on what worked and what didn't

How can we be more specific? What else did any of the earlier lecture’s “Futurists” do to more clearly articulate their futures?

Refine ideas of the future that may not have been realized well by the tools.
Explore ways to be more specific in future prompts.
Discuss the potential for creating more objects or icons that contribute to the future narrative.
Examine the types of stories that AI tools use to generate the future from.

≈15 Minutes

Project in front of class, students still can look around on their own in Figma. Again, collect the group’s answers to what was working/what wasn’t and how to improve.

Comment/edit figma “pages”

### 6. Identify shared themes among the students 

Split into small groups based on shared themes. 

Collaborate to develop clearer visual metaphors for your shared themes.
Work together to create a number of different objects that can be rendered in more specific, controllable ways.

≈10 Minutes

We’ll try to generate a few unique themes and group the students into those themes based on their preferred futures.

Students then work together in their small groups coming up with an object or specific place for each of them to spend some more time trying to create.

### 7. Collaboratively render new, more specific objects individually

Each student in a group should render their own object, then work together to group the images to create a more understandable picture of your shared future

Prioritize the most important things to signal from each group's future and encourage visualization and condensing ideas into specific, describable objects or concepts that point towards your preferences for the future.

Look at your objects together and discuss what works/doesn’t
how can thinking more about being clear about the future be valuable more in works?
how are/aren’t these machine learning tools useful
where/how they could be more useful!?

≈20–50 Minutes (go until 630 or 7pm?)
spend about 10 more minutes on the text-to-image output
spend the rest of the time collecting them and quickly viewing everyone’s results.
if we’re going until 7, then maybe 15 minutes could be spent rendering new images.

New figma pages to collect new object renders.
Same process — please save the prompts and tools used for each image.

Work “alone together” to write new prompts for your objects, then work together to try and collect your objects in a meaningful way.

### 8. Conclusion; Workshop Objectives

Introduce knowledge of future studies and provide ideas and concepts for thinking about and deciphering preferable futures.

Provide practice using text-to-image machine learning tools.
Foster an understanding of how these tools work.
Critique limitations (and how these might reflect limitations [and opportunities] for working with and within society as a whole)

Encourage critical thinking about the status quo of design and the potential for both design and machine learning tools in the future.

Discuss semiotic signs and ways to signal or direct culture to the best possible futures.
