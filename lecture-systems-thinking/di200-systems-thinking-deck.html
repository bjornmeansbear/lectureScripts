Systems and services deal in abstract, hidden, invisible realms.

We have to train our brains, and our processes, to see them — and then reveal them.

Definition

Service design

The whole experience, and the operation behind it.

Not letting it just happen accidentally. What they need, at the right time, in the right place.

Definition

Touchpoint

A moment the person who needs the service actually meets it.

The bag on the door. The “we lost a sock” text. The folded stack coming back.

A guest entering the lobby
  • —The revolving door
  • —An agent at the front desk — or not
  • —Signs to their room, or the restaurant
  • —How things smell and feel

They move people along at the pace and quality we wish.

Same desk, different person, different service.

Change any one of these things and you've changed the service.

What lasts is often what adapts. And how a system adapts comes down to its feedback loops.

Definition

Systems design

How we design with, or for, systems: the rules, flows and feedback loops that drive outcomes.

We often can't control the externality. We can control how things respond to it.

Definition

Feedback loop

Something happens and changes the system. Then that change feeds back in.

A well-planned loop has a limiter that keeps it from cycling out of control.

Reinforcing

Feeds itself. Every lap worse.

Balancing

A thermostat. Has a limiter, settles back.

In a hotel, what could throw how a guest checks in out of whack?
