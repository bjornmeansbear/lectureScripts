# Part A — The briefing

## 1 · Why this site is not a neutral site (3 min)

Deliver this flat. No drama; the facts are enough.

> Before anyone goes anywhere, you need to know what building you're walking into.
>
> The City of Baltimore borrowed $301 million to build this hotel. It opened in 2008 to prop up the convention center next door. Since then the city has covered more than $140 million in operating losses. The public owns it and the public pays for it.
>
> In 2024 the workers there went on strike — the first strike in the building's history — and won a new contract.
>
> So the people you're about to meet work in a building that is publicly owned, politically contested, and organized. That's not trivia. It changes what it means for a college student to walk up with a notebook.

Then the point that everything else hangs on:

> **The Hilton agreed to host this course. No individual worker agreed to anything.**
>
> Management granting us access is not consent from the person mopping the lobby. Consent is not transitive. You get it yourself, from each person, every time, or you don't have it.

## 2 · The power problem (3 min)

> Here's the thing I most need you to understand, and it's the thing students get wrong.
>
> You will show up in a group, with a faculty member, having been let in by management, carrying notebooks and cameras. To a housekeeper on the clock, you look like you're with the company. You are not a peer asking a favor. You read as someone who might report back.
>
> Which means when you ask a question, **"no" is expensive for them and cheap for you.** They may not feel free to refuse. If someone talks to you, you cannot assume they wanted to.
>
> That asymmetry is your responsibility to manage. Not theirs.

Three rules that follow, and say that they follow from it:

1. **Make refusal easy and say so out loud.** "You can say no, it's completely fine" is part of the ask, not a nicety.
2. **Never approach someone mid-task.** Not while they're cleaning a room, carrying something, or with a guest. You can get a person in trouble with a supervisor, and they'll pay for that long after you've graduated.
3. **One "no" ends it.** No rephrasing, no second attempt later, no asking their coworker the same question hoping for a better result.

## 3 · The consent script (2 min)

They memorize this. Three sentences, and rehearse them aloud in Wk1 or on the train.

> **"Hi — I'm a design student at MICA. We're doing a class project about how this building works, and I'd love to hear how your job actually goes if you have a few minutes.**
>
> **Nothing you say goes to management with your name on it, and you can stop any time or tell me to go away.**
>
> **Is now okay, or is there a better time?"**

What each sentence is doing — tell them, so they can improvise without losing the parts that matter:

- Sentence one: **who you are, why you're there, what you want.** No jargon. Never say "ethnographic research" or "user research" to someone at work.
- Sentence two: **the two things that make it consent** — what happens to their words, and that they can stop.
- Sentence three: **hands them control of the timing**, which is the one thing they actually can control on a shift.

> If you can't say those three sentences without reading them, you're not ready to talk to anybody. Practice on each other.

## 4 · What you may and may not record (2 min)

**Yes:**
- Spaces, objects, wear, traces, hand-written signs, layouts
- Your own drawings, always
- Quotes, unattributed or attributed only by role — "a front desk agent said…"
- Counts, durations, sequences

**No:**
- **Faces.** Not in the background, not "it's fine, they're blurry."
- **Name badges**, name tags, or anything with a person's name on it
- **Guest information** of any kind — room numbers, registration screens, luggage tags, anything on a monitor
- **Recording audio without explicitly asking**, as its own separate question
- Anything in a back-of-house area you weren't specifically shown

> If you're not sure, don't, and ask me. A photo you didn't take costs you nothing. A photo of someone's name badge on a class blog can cost them a job.

## 5 · The strike, and things that aren't yours to ask (1 min)

This is the one most likely to go wrong, and most likely to be well-intentioned.

> Some of the people you talk to were on that picket line in 2024. Some weren't and have opinions about the ones who were.
>
> **Do not ask people about the strike, the union, or their contract.** Not because it isn't interesting — it's the most interesting thing about this building — but because a worker discussing labor relations with a stranger who came in through management is being asked to take a risk for your class project.
>
> If someone raises it themselves, listen, don't push, and don't take notes on the specifics of who said what about whom.
>
> The civic and labor dimension of this site is real and it's a legitimate design subject. You research it through public records, reporting, and city budget documents — not by putting a housekeeper on the spot in a hallway.

## 6 · Guests are not a captive audience (1 min)

- **Never interrupt a transaction.** Not at the desk, not at the bar, not mid-check-in.
- Approach only people who are visibly waiting or at rest, and open with the same script.
- A guest who is annoyed with the hotel may want to vent at you. Fine. But you're not their complaint line and don't promise anything gets fixed.
- **Never follow anyone.** Not to an elevator, not down a corridor, not "just to see where they go."

## 7 · Extraction, and what goes back (2 min)

End on this. It's the part they'll remember.

> Be honest about the shape of this. You are taking people's time and their knowledge of their own workplace, and converting it into a grade and a portfolio piece. They get nothing automatically.
>
> That's extraction. It's not automatically wrong — it's how most research works — but it is *something you owe against*, and the debt is real.
>
> So, two obligations. First: **be worth talking to.** Show up prepared, don't waste their time, don't ask what you could have found out yourself.
>
> Second: at the end of this semester we present to Hilton staff. Not to management, to staff. The test I'm going to hold your final work to is whether the person who gave you twenty minutes in September would look at it in December and think their time was well spent. If the answer is no, it doesn't matter how good the object is.

## 8 · When something goes wrong (1 min)

Give them the moves, because in the moment they'll freeze.

- **Someone gets upset:** stop, apologize once, don't explain yourself, leave. Tell me the same day.
- **Someone discloses something serious** — harassment, safety, immigration, a grievance: listen, don't take notes, don't repeat it, don't put it in your fieldnotes. Tell me. It is not course material.
- **A manager stops you:** be polite, say what class you're with, come find me. Don't negotiate.
- **You're asked to leave anywhere:** leave immediately. No exceptions, no clarifying questions.
- **You realize you've done something wrong:** tell me. Nobody's grade has ever been hurt by reporting a mistake here. It will be hurt by hiding one.

## Fieldwork Ground Rules — Hilton Baltimore Inner Harbor

**The hotel agreed to host us. No individual agreed to anything. You get consent yourself, every time.**

### Say this

> "Hi — I'm a design student at MICA. We're doing a class project about how this building works, and I'd love to hear how your job actually goes if you have a few minutes. Nothing you say goes to management with your name on it, and you can stop any time or tell me to go away. Is now okay, or is there a better time?"

### Always

- Ask first. Say why. Say they can stop.
- Approach people at rest, never mid-task.
- Take one "no" as final — no rephrasing, no asking their coworker instead.
- Attribute by role, never by name: "a front desk agent said…"
- Write down what you saw. Leave out what would identify anyone.

### Never

- Photograph **faces**, **name badges**, or **guest information** of any kind
- Record audio without asking as a separate question
- Interrupt a transaction with a guest
- Follow anyone
- Ask about **the strike, the union, or anyone's contract**
- Enter a back-of-house area you weren't shown
- Promise that anything will be fixed

### If something goes wrong

Someone's upset → stop, apologize once, leave, tell Kristian that day. Someone discloses something serious → listen, take no notes, tell Kristian. Not course material. Asked to leave → leave immediately. You made a mistake → tell Kristian. Reporting it costs you nothing. Hiding it doesn't.

### The standard for the whole semester

> Would the person who gave you twenty minutes in September look at your final project in December and think their time was well spent?
