# Some Semiotics — shirt

## The brief

MSCHF's big red boot -> what's the tshirt conceptual equivalent to be a fun front, that then has a big networked explainer diagram on the back talking about saussure, barthes, berger, duvall, pierce, etc.?

Plus: teach a little semiotics on the back. Boot Boyz Biz is the model.

## The Format

Three parts:

1. **Front** — a graphic. Read in about a second, across a room.
2. **Back** — a diagram or explainer. Read at close range.
3. **The product page** — a long research essay.

**The essay already exists.** The lecture script is 3,383 spoken words. That is the product description, near enough that it needs editing rather than writing.

---

## The back — the teaching diagram

This is the same for either front. Build it from real historical diagrams rather than inventing a network:

- **Barthes's myth diagram** as the spine. The staggered two-tier table where the whole sign of the first system becomes the signifier of the second. It is the best diagram in semiotics and it does §10 by itself.
- **Saussure's dyad** feeding in — signifier over signified, the oval.
- **Peirce's triad** beside it — representamen / object / interpretant.
- **Ogden & Richards' triangle** (1923) if there's room — thought / word / thing.
- **Duvall's three columns** as the contemporary read: Form / Content / Context, Syntax / Semantics / Pragmatics, Symbolic / Iconic / Indexical. Deck slide `4003`. This is the one that makes the shirt current rather than historical.

Chair and cat as the worked example running through it, so the diagram has objects in it and not only terms.

## Front A — the care label

The laundry symbols from the shirt's own care label, printed 10–12 inches. The real ones, for that real garment.

Everyone has carried this against their skin their whole life and nobody taught them the system. It's the purest symbolic sign in the lecture — there is nothing in a triangle that is about bleach. Real functional information, printed at the size of a statement, in a language the wearer doesn't speak.

The Whannel line as an object: things we already know, in a language we will never understand. Don't print the quote. The shirt does it.

Front poses it, back decodes it — which is deck slides `1001` → `1002` exactly.

**Optional cruelty.** One symbol is invented. Undetectable, which is §3.

**Source slides.** `1000`, `1001`, `1002`.

## Front B — the shirt icon

The Noun Project t-shirt glyph, printed at 1:1 on an actual t-shirt. The flat, square-shouldered, straight-hemmed silhouette no real shirt has ever had. It can never line up — real shirts have necklines and drape and the icon doesn't. That gap is the product.

The Big Red Boot structure exactly: an icon of a symbol. It doesn't resemble a shirt, it resembles the *drawing convention* for a shirt, which was already arbitrary. Same move MSCHF made in rubber for three hundred and fifty dollars, made in cotton for twenty.

**Source slides.** `3000`–`3002` (Big Red Boot), `418` (Kosuth), the stray `2006` at the end of the Peirce row (McCloud's Big Triangle).

## The trade

B is the better gag A is the better shirt, because the information on it is true, so it stays interesting once the joke is spent. A also earns its back diagram; B's front and back are making the same point twice.

---

## Production

One-color screenprint. Dark brown ink, not black, on a warm natural ground rather than white — the standing pairing.

The back has to survive being printed small and read close: solid rules, no hairlines, no tonal fills, nothing that depends on halftone.

## Rights, before drawing anything

Boot Boyz bootlegs on purpose. That isn't the house position, so check these rather than inherit their stance:

- **Care symbols** are an ISO 3758 / GINETEX standard set, and GINETEX holds trademarks on them in some jurisdictions. Check before tracing.
- **Barthes's myth diagram** — *Mythologies* is 1957 and Barthes died in 1980, so it is still in copyright. A redrawn diagram of a conceptual structure is a different thing from a facsimile; redraw, don't scan.
- **Peirce and Ogden & Richards** are safely out of copyright.
- **Duvall's table** is a living author's work. Ask him. He'd probably say yes, and the lecture already credits him twice.
- **Noun Project icons** are CC BY — attribution goes on the garment or the label, not just the website.
