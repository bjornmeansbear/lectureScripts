The Affect of Ideologies on Designed Forms.

In looking to artists and designers from previous movements, innovators we’ve historically documented are known for novel visuals, but arrived at their novel visuals through exploring (new?) ideologic territory; experimenting with different ways of thinking. Avant garde visuals stemming from these movements were the result of the ideas (or ideals) to come out of their novel thinking, not really the main ideas themselves. Our past is not about style for style’s sake, but style that illuminates a theoretical position (or at least style that is arrived at from a thought process, from a set of values, or from some set of hypotheses).

Study aesthetics and form making methods from multiple time periods, cultures, and ideologies. How might new forms be derived from these? what ideologies are these times/cultures/etc. working from? where do ideologies overlap?

Initial discussion points for independent study planning.
- Historicizing and contextualizing aesthetics and those aesthetics origins
- Aesthetics are not just visual phenomena, the ideas that go into their production are just as important.
- Explore how the contexts and content of a project lead to unique form generation. 
- Investigate multiple processes for arriving at formal outcomes.
- Understand the mechanical workings of aesthetic frameworks and how these inner workings dictate the affect of an aesthetic in the world.
- Understand and be able to demonstrate the ways in which aesthetics and forms are holistic, political phenomena involving an expansive range of productive forces and outputs.
- Understand how to synthesize an aesthetic from a range of sources to express an ideological viewpoint.
- Keep a process archive to document discoveries, paths of thought, and ideation throughout the course of the study.
- Perhaps an important formal method for this study is to combine, compare, and derive new forms and works from existing ones from throughout history?
