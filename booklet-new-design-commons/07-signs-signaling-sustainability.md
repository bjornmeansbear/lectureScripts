# Signs Signaling Sustainability

<!--

The argument:
  - Modernism is treated as synonymous with "good graphic design." Every company
    rebranding right now uses humanist geometric sans-serif. That's fine, except
    it's the ONLY criteria we have for formal goodness.
  - Alain de Botton: if you find a design beautiful, it's because your values are
    reflected in it.
  - Therefore, if the value is all life flourishing, then SUSTAINABLE DESIGN IS
    BEAUTIFUL DESIGN — and the non-sustainable becomes ugly.
  - What's good about this as an aesthetic direction: it doesn't say what the thing
    should LOOK like, only what it should SAY. It indexes values, not taste.
  - The honest problem it raises: beauty isn't fixed, and Western history is full
    of people saying so.

The four:
  1. Amager Bakke vapor ring (Bjarke Ingels Group, Copenhagen) — a ring puffed per
     ton of CO2. Never built; the person who was going to help was arrested in a
     Danish scandal. Makes an immaterial thing material.
  2. Reverberation crosswalks (Graham Coreil-Allen, Baltimore) — paint on asphalt as
     graphic design; each one a step toward a walkable city.
     [VERIFY the project name — Coreil-Allen's practice is usually "New Public Sites"]
  3. solar.lowtechmagazine.com — bitmapped images, high resolution at low file size,
     recolorable per context. Low power as a design constraint that is legible AS design.
  4. DC High Water Mark Project (Curry Hackett) — stanchions marking floods that
     have happened and floods that are predicted. You see the water without the flood.
