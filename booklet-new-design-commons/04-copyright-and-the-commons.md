# Copyright and the Commons

**This essay is already written.** See [`workshop-semiotic-commons/copyright-and-the-commons.md`](../workshop-semiotic-commons/copyright-and-the-commons.md) — finished, footnoted, publication-ready. Nothing here should replace it.

It runs: the Originis myth of the Author → copyright briefly (Berne, 1710, life plus seventy, ninety-five for corporations) → idea vs. expression via the chairness problem → fair use, Fairey and Warhol → the public domain and its re-enclosure problem, via Disney's Snow White → Ostrom on why commons actually fail → Stallman, Raymond, Lessig → where to find the material → why designers should care (Venezuela; and the argument that public domain sources force you to build meaning rather than borrow one).

## What it needs to become a booklet chapter

**Trim the workshop scaffolding.** It opens as *"a primer for running this workshop — or any workshop like it — without me in the room"* and closes with *"Now go cut something up."* Both are right for a workshop handout and wrong for a chapter. The closing line before them — "Everything is a remix" — is the real ending.

**Resolve the overlap with essays 01 and 03.** Stallman's printer story appears here and in 01. The Cathedral and the Bazaar appears here and in 01. Vernacular architecture and *How Buildings Learn* appear here and in 03. Right now the same material is doing service in three chapters.

Suggested division: **01** owns Stallman and the four freedoms as the argument about tools. **03** owns vernacular and remix as the argument about culture. **04** owns the legal mechanics — what copyright actually is, what fair use actually covers, what the licenses actually do — and cross-references the other two rather than re-telling them. That means cutting the Stallman and Raymond paragraphs here down to a sentence each with a pointer back.

**Keep the fairy-tale opener.** The mythic register at the top and the drop into plain prose right after is doing real work, and it's the only place in the booklet that opens that way.

**Decide about the mid-essay chair sequence.** Four chairs, each a different expression of chairness. It's the clearest explanation of idea-vs-expression in the whole repo. It also connects to the Oka Terra Chair thread flagged in `SHARED-COMPONENTS.md` for the circular-economy workshops.
