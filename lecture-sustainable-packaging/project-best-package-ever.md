# The Best Package Ever

Final project, Package Design (GD365) · first run at MICA, Spring 2014 · goes with [Intro to Sustainable Packaging](README.md) · CC BY-SA 4.0

Five weeks.

## The brief

This is the final project. You have several weeks; use them wisely. Pick whatever you want to package (yourself, something for your senior project, your favorite condiment, whatever) and then make it the most amazingly packaged version of that thing we've ever seen.

The goal is not just the nicest-looking label. Consider the package as a whole system. Come up with forms, distribution, primary, secondary and tertiary packaging (if needed), ideas for materials, how it could be made for real. Make it amazing in its story, its prototyping, its planning, its research, its design. Think about everything we've talked about in terms of what a “package” is.

Blow me and your peers away. Go.

## Week by week

**Weeks 1–2: decide what you're packaging.** Answer these:

- What is the product?
- Who is the target consumer?
- Where will it be sold?
- What environment will it compete in?
- What price point?
- What are the cost constraints?
- What distribution methods are planned?
- How will it be transported?
- How and where will it be stored?
- How does it need to be protected?
- How will it be displayed?

Once you have some answers, think about these:

- Can an existing structure be re-engineered?
- Can a new structure be chosen from a stock source?
- Does a new structure need to be developed?
- Should the structure be proprietary?
- What is the structure made of?

And this:

- What is the category competition? Document it.
- How do you set your product apart from it?

Bring the answers, plus the beginnings of your visual thinking, as a mood board: typeface samples, color palettes, reference imagery, other products at the same price point or for the same consumer. Whatever captures the personality of the product and the person buying it. A board, a folder of junk, anything you can show the class.

**Weeks 2–3: rough drafts.** Sketches and mockups, plus the first experiments with the physical structures you're going to tackle.

**Weeks 3–4: near-final.** Near-final designs, visual and physical. Present them to a small group of peers.

**Weeks 4–5: finals.** Final critique. Then document and send the final work (revised if you want) by the end of that week.

## Notes for reuse

- The 2014 syllabus ended with a project epilogue that's worth keeping in front of students: “I think these projects will allow you to explore how ‘packaging’ and ‘graphic design’ fit together without me prescribing forms or solutions to you. The choices are yours to make. You have my permission to fail. No one learns anything just trying safe, easy stuff.”
- The question list in weeks 1–2 is the most reusable part of the whole course. It works as a checklist for any packaging project.
