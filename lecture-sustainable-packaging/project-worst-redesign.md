# Worst Re-Design

Project 1, Package Design (GD365) · first run at MICA, Spring 2014 · goes with [Intro to Sustainable Packaging](README.md) · follows [Best/Worst](project-best-worst.md) · CC BY-SA 4.0

Five weeks, plus one for documentation.

## The brief

Use the “worst” package from Best/Worst (or go find another one, now that you know you'll be redesigning it). Describe why it is bad. Think about it from several angles, because you are going to develop a better solution, and it can't just be a better *visual* solution. It has to be a truly better packaging solution: better for protecting, better for informing, better for selling, better for shipping, better for its life cycle. Better at everything.

Investigate alternative materials. Think about who owns the package. Think about changes in handling, packing, display and advertising. Changes in, well, everything. For example: milkmen used to pick up the empty bottles, so the customer never owned the packaging. Today most of us own the packaging the moment we buy the product. How could that change?

Beautiful graphics on a sturdy box might not be the best new solution, or even a necessary one. Ask producers what they need. Look at how the item is made and shipped. Other ideas may show up along those lines of inquiry.

This is a research project as much as a design project. The final design (the package and the process behind it) should show that research. Design a process, not just a product.

## Week by week

**Week 1: research.** Choose the package. Investigate, as well as you can, the larger ecosystem the product is part of. Bring an image of the product and be ready to explain what you found.

- How and where does it get produced?
- How does it get to the store?
- How is it presented in the store?
- How is it sold?
- How do customers interact with it, in the store and at home?
- What happens to the packaging once it's home and unpacked?
- What do other companies selling the same product do?
- Are there different existing ways to make, transport, sell and package this product?
- If it's part of a larger brand, how does it fit the brand's story?
- How does this packaging play into the “needs” vs. “wants” conversation?

A side note: you're designers, so everything you do is a chance for another design project. Treat the research as one. The way you collect it, the way you format it: can that be designed too, so it helps you see something?

**Week 2: directions.** Turn the research into design directions. Investigate materials and forms alongside them.

**Week 3: mockup.** A physical mockup at more or less the planned scale. It can be a blank paperboard form. Bring printouts or a PDF of draft visuals too.

**Week 4: critique.** Make last week's mockups shine. We'll look at them as finals, but you'll still have time to improve and document them.

**Week 5: final critique.**

**Week 6: documentation.** Final photo documentation due, with any revisions. One last quick crit.

## Requirements

1. The worst package, chosen.
2. A physical mockup of the final design.
3. A dieline showing how the design could actually be manufactured.
4. A written explanation of the design decisions in terms of Protect, Inform, Sell, plus the research behind them.
5. Documentation photos of the mockup.
