# Package Design: More Than Just Boxes (GD365, Spring 2014)

The course the projects in this folder come from. MICA, Mondays 4–10pm, one semester. Kept here as an outline for anyone rebuilding the course; the MICA policy boilerplate is left out. CC BY-SA 4.0.

## What the course was

An advanced studio on how typography, illustration and design behave on (or as) three-dimensional forms and different materials. Students worked individually on packaging concepts organized around the three jobs of a package: **Protect, Inform, Sell**. Research, highly crafted comps, and professional presentation were all part of it, and the course paid steady attention to social, sustainable and environmental questions.

The construction side stayed basic on purpose: scissors, X-Acto knives, rulers, tape, glue, paperboard. The point was that any designer, not only a career package designer, should be able to mock up a package well. Students who wanted to could use the fabrication lab (laser cutting, 3D printing).

## Learning outcomes

Students would be able to:

1. Analyze existing package designs, visually and physically.
2. Develop packaging systems as an individual.
3. Identify what a package's design needs to do.
4. Show design intent and production skills.
5. Organize and time-manage a package design project.
6. Show a working knowledge of package design and production issues.
7. Prepare for packaging work professionally, or at least for the occasional packaging project.

## The semester, roughly

| Week | In class |
|---|---|
| 1 | Intro. Why are you here, what's your mockup experience. Lecture: what packaging design is and what we'll cover. Assign Best/Worst. In class: make a box from one 8.5×11 sheet, no glue or tape, cut and fold only, and it has to stay closed. |
| 2 | Look at Best/Worst. Start Project 1. **Demo 1: score and fold** (basic box). Lecture: what a dieline is, and other basics of construction. In class: draw the dieline for last week's box. |
| 3 | **Demo 2: corners**, multi-layer platforms. Lecture: research as a design tool. Project 1 research presentations. |
| 4 | **Demo 3: curved surfaces.** Lecture: asking the right questions. Project 1 refined research and drafts. |
| 5 | **Demo 4: wrapping.** Project 1 draft comps. |
| 6 | Project 1 critique. Introduce Project 2. |
| 7 | Project 1 documentation. Lecture: the best package is no package. Digital packaging presentation. |
| 8 | Spring break |
| 9 | Work day. Another wrapping demo. |
| 10 | Project 2 critique. Super complex boxes. |
| 11 | Packaging history. Introduce Project 3. Project 2 documentation due. |
| 12 | Project 3 progress. Package Cooker. |
| 13–14 | Work days. Near-final critique in small groups. |
| 15 | Final critique. Final documentation due that week. |

(2014 also lost a week to snow, so the order above is tidied from what actually happened.)

## Materials

X-Acto and plenty of replacement blades, bone folder, PVA, a few tapes (masking, clear, packing), rulers and squares, cutting mat, a range of paper and board stocks, maybe a hot glue gun and a compass. Spray adhesive only outside or in a ventilated spray booth.
