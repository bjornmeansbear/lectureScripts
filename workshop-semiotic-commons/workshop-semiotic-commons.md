# MAKE IT MEAN SOMETHING

*A two-day workshop on semiotics, the commons, and making do*

> Working title. Alternates at bottom.

---

## The pitch

You can make almost anything say what you want. You don't need the right image or the
right text. You need a pile of material and a willingness to cut it apart.

Students build a shared image library, then use it — and only it — to retell a
public-domain folktale twice. Once as an eight-page zine. Once as a poster.

---

## Day one — the zine

Short lecture: my own work, the design commons, and how meaning gets made.

Everyone contributes images to a common pile. We print it grayscale. Each student
collages an eight-page zine retelling the story. No words.

At the end, the maker flips the pages while someone else reads the zine aloud. The maker
says nothing. Whatever the reader finds in the images is what the zine says. Sometimes
that matches what the maker intended. Often it doesn't. Barthes wrote an essay about this.
Here it happens in front of you, in a room, in about four minutes.

---

## Day two — the poster

One poster, built from imagery already used in the zines — yours or anyone else's. The
original sources are closed. Type is allowed now, and digital tools.

The poster is an aphorism. Whatever moral survived the retelling, said in one gesture.
(The term is Elliott Earls'.)

Zine and poster print back to back. Unfolds into one object.

---

## Who it's for

Undergraduate studio, junior or senior. Graduate seminar. 12–20 students.

## What they leave with

Two finished pieces. A working understanding of how signs carry meaning and how that
meaning shifts under reuse. A practical argument for the public domain, made by using it.

## What you need

- Two full days
- Studio space with wall room
- Grayscale laser printing, high volume
- Scissors, glue, tape
- Day two: computers, large-format output

## Fee

$2,000–$3,000 plus travel and materials. Single-day version available.

## Prior versions

- University of Utah, January 2025 — first run. Steamboat Willie stills and Victorian clip art, public domain. The Ash Lad Who Had an Eating Match with a Troll as source text.
- MICA Baltimore, My own classes, January 2025, August 2025 — second and third runs, same folktale and image set. Started a companion zine documenting the workshop (unfinished, see `variations-on-a-norwegian-folktale.md`).
- Germany, October 2025 — evolved from the above. Participants' own family photographs as the commons instead of the Steamboat willy based initial image sets.
