# DRAWDOWN DIPTYCHS

*A poster workshop where the source material is a real climate solution, not a folktale*

> Working title, matching the existing class project name. Alternates at bottom.

---

## The pitch

Same commons-and-collage method as the semiotic zine workshop, aimed at a different kind of meaning-making: instead of retelling a folktale, students research one real climate solution from Project Drawdown, then argue with it visually — two posters, in dialogue or in tension with each other, built entirely from public domain and Creative Commons material.

This is the companion workshop to [Semiotic Zine + Poster](workshop-semiotic-commons.md) — same commons, same constraint, but the content comes from climate science instead of a story, and the output is a two-poster diptych instead of a zine-and-poster pair. Bookable on its own, or added onto the semiotic workshop for departments that want both.

---

## Friday — pick a solution, gather the pile

Short lecture: how Project Drawdown ranks and explains real climate solutions, and how public domain and Creative Commons sources (Flickr Commons, RawPixel, public.work) plus open type (Google Fonts, Velvetyne) become raw material for arguing about one of them visually.

Each student picks one solution from [Drawdown's list](https://www.drawdown.org/solutions) and researches it past the surface — where it's being done, who's doing it, what it would actually take to scale. Out of that: a list of keywords, a few aphorisms or quotes that say something the Drawdown page doesn't, and a fast, wide image-gathering pass. Literal objects, metaphors, unexpected allusions, whatever seems to relate.

Friday ends with everyone holding a pile of material, not a plan.

## Saturday — metaphors, then posters

We start by going around the room. What did you pick, and how did you get from a solution to an image that isn't a picture of that solution? That conversation is the actual content of the workshop. A wind farm is easy to photograph and hard to mean anything with.

Then rapid experimental compositions — small, fast, disposable studies exploring type alone, image alone, and the two combined. Go wide before going deep. Nothing here is precious. Poster building starts in the afternoon, from the strongest experiments.

## Sunday — finish, then hang it

Keep building. Two posters that put one solution in dialogue with itself, complementary or contrasting, reader's choice. Each needs real hierarchy (a heading plus at least two more levels), at least 200 words, and at least one image. What a "poster" is stays open — a print piece, a website, an animation, a building, whatever the solution suggests.

Everything goes up on the wall by end of day. The final crit is an exhibition, not a table review: does the tension between the two posters say something, or do they repeat each other in different clothes?

**Two-day version:** Friday and Saturday collapse into one long day. It works, and the thing that suffers is the metaphor conversation on Saturday morning, which is the part worth protecting.

## Who it's for

Undergraduate studio, any level. Graduate seminar. 12–20 students. Works well paired with the semiotic zine workshop for a department that wants a longer visit, or as its own standalone weekend.

## What they leave with

A two-poster diptych built entirely from open-licensed material, arguing something real about a specific climate solution. A working research habit — go past the summary page, find the aphorism, find the argument — applied to visual form. A concrete answer to whether meaning about something as abstract as "climate solutions" can actually be made legible through pure design choices (grid, hierarchy, material) and not just through text.

## What you need

- A weekend, Friday through Sunday. Two-day and multi-week versions both exist — see Prior versions
- Studio space, wall room for critique
- Access to Flickr Commons, RawPixel's public domain section, public.work, Google Fonts, Adobe Fonts, Velvetyne
- Print output for at least the final diptych (11×17 recommended, though format is negotiable per the "what even is a poster" prompt)

## Fee

$2,000–$3,000 plus travel and materials, matching the semiotic workshop. Add $500–$1,000 if booked as a joint two-workshop visit with Semiotic Zine + Poster. Full embedded-course version: inquire. Four to five weeks inside an existing class is where this started, and it needs a real conversation about format before pricing.

## Prior versions

- **University of Utah, 2021 — the first run.** Five weeks, embedded in a class, and taught **entirely remotely**. Worth knowing: the whole method survives being run over video. The pile is a shared drive instead of a table, and the final crit is a screen full of posters instead of a wall.
- **MICA, as a four-week unit.** Assigned week one. Students return week two with research and mood boards, and poster work starts in class that day. Poster drafts week three. Finals week four. Full syllabus, week-by-week deliverables, and a poster-designer inspiration list (Anette Lenz, Art Sims, Bráulio Amado, Daniel Eatock, Emory Douglas, Jean Benoit Levy, Philippe Apeloig, Shun Ishizuka, Sindiso Nyoni, Skolos and Wedell, Vincent Perrottet, Yve Ludwig) are in `drawdown-diptych-full-syllabus.md`, this folder.
- The weekend and two-day versions above compress that unit. Not yet run in the short form.

## Notes to self

- This is genuinely two products, same as circular economy ended up being: the **sprint** (a weekend, standalone or paired with the semiotic workshop) and the **full embedded unit** (four to five weeks, a real class takeover, priced and scoped differently). Keep both live rather than picking one — a department with only a guest-visit budget wants the sprint; a department that wants to hand over real curriculum wants the full unit.
- The unit has been taught at two lengths now, five weeks and four. If a department asks what the minimum is, four weeks is proven and the weekend is not yet.
- Worth deciding whether to fold this in as a formal second offering under workshop #1, or keep it fully independent on the menu. Leaning toward: independent, since it stands on its own and the sustainability crossover gives it a different pitch angle (works for design departments *and* for sustainability/environmental studies programs, which #1 alone doesn't reach).
