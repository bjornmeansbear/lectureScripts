# DRAWDOWN DIPTYCHS

*A poster workshop where the source material is a real climate solution, not a folktale*

> Working title, matching the existing class project name. Alternates at bottom.

---

## The pitch

Same commons-and-collage method as the semiotic zine workshop, aimed at a different kind of meaning-making: instead of retelling a folktale, students research one real climate solution from Project Drawdown, then argue with it visually — two posters, in dialogue or in tension with each other, built entirely from public domain and Creative Commons material.

This is the companion workshop to [Semiotic Zine + Poster](workshop-semiotic-commons.md) — same commons, same constraint, but the content comes from climate science instead of a story, and the output is a two-poster diptych instead of a zine-and-poster pair. Bookable on its own, or as a second day added onto the semiotic workshop for departments that want both.

---

## Day one — pick a solution, gather the pile

Short lecture: how Project Drawdown ranks and explains real climate solutions, and how public domain/CC image sources (Flickr Commons, RawPixel, public.work) plus open type (Google Fonts, Velvetyne) become raw material for arguing about one of them visually, the same way the semiotic workshop's shared image pile works.

Each student picks one solution from [Drawdown's list](https://www.drawdown.org/solutions) and researches it past the surface — where it's being done, who's doing it, what it would actually take to scale. From that research: a list of keywords, a few aphorisms or quotes that say something about the solution beyond its Drawdown page, and a fast, wide image-gathering pass from open sources — literal objects, metaphors, unexpected allusions, whatever seems to relate.

Students move straight into rapid experimental compositions — small, fast, disposable studies exploring type alone, image alone, and type and image combined. Go wide before going deep. Nothing here is precious.

## Day two — the diptych

Working from the strongest experiments, students build two posters that put their solution in dialogue with itself — complementary or contrasting points of view, reader's choice. Each poster needs real hierarchy (a heading plus at least two more levels), a real amount of text (200 words minimum), and at least one image, but the actual form a "poster" takes is deliberately open — a traditional print piece, a website, an animation, a building, whatever the solution itself suggests.

Close with a group critique, reading the diptychs the way the semiotic workshop reads its zines: does the tension between the two posters actually say something, or do they just repeat each other in different clothes?

## Who it's for

Undergraduate studio, any level. Graduate seminar. 12–20 students. Works well paired with the semiotic zine workshop for a department that wants a longer visit, or as its own standalone two days.

## What they leave with

A two-poster diptych built entirely from open-licensed material, arguing something real about a specific climate solution. A working research habit — go past the summary page, find the aphorism, find the argument — applied to visual form. A concrete answer to whether meaning about something as abstract as "climate solutions" can actually be made legible through pure design choices (grid, hierarchy, material) and not just through text.

## What you need

- Two full days for the sprint version (below); see Prior Versions for the full multi-week format this compresses
- Studio space, wall room for critique
- Access to Flickr Commons, RawPixel's public domain section, public.work, Google Fonts, Adobe Fonts, Velvetyne
- Print output for at least the final diptych (11×17 recommended, though format is negotiable per the "what even is a poster" prompt)
