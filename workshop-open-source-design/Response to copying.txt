Response to copying ...

Not that this is the best forum for discussion, but just thought I'd try to chime in with my Libre Designer perspective... 

What this project seems to suppose is that "copying is sometimes okay" is opposite of "copying is always wrong" — but "copying is sometimes okay" still implies that it isn't okay to copy in plenty of circumstances. This misses the actual opposite, 

"COPYING IS ALWAYS RIGHT!" 

Copying is never ethically wrong! The history of cultural production is about copying. Learning new skills is about copying. The the fact that "styles" or "trends" exist at all requires copying. Vernacular designing is based on copying. The entire F/LOS ecosystem thrives BECAUSE of a "please copy me" mentality. Copying is always *ethically right,* it just frequently happens to be *legally wrong* under our current systems (these systems also create the false illusions of "creativity" and "originality" for makers to aspire to). 

Is copying right or wrong isn't a moral problem, it is one of power dynamics. Those with power are the most likely to desire your not copying; but have no qualms with appropriating the less powerful's production. That is a real opposite, and isn't presented here (at least in my superficial ability to read this solely as an instagram post).

>>>>>>>>

Modernist furniture is a great example of this. Those who funded the "development" of famous chairs maintain exclusive license to manufacture them at high markup. This defeats the "best for the most for the least" mantra that these, at least the Eamses, designs were made with. Ikea, as sort of a modernist "copy machine," reaches this ideal much more closely; the actual "fakers" are even better at it (well maybe not the "best" part, but certainly the most for the least...).
