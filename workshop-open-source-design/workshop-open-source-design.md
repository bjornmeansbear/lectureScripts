# THE LIBRE DESIGNER

*A workshop on open source, the commons, and refusing to rent your tools*

> Working title, matching the existing [case study](https://a.wjerk.shop/case-study-libre-designer.html) on a.wjerk.shop. Alternates at bottom.

---

## The pitch

The libre designer is a utopian character — a way of working where nothing you depend on can be locked away from you. Free and libre open source tools, openly licensed fonts and images, and a design practice built on the same logic that's always driven culture: copy, improve, share back.

Students build something real — a poster, a small publication, an identity system — using only F/LOSS tools and openly licensed material, start to finish. Then they give it back: license their own work the same way, and publish it somewhere public.

---

## Day one — libre, not gratis

Short lecture, adapted from the [Libre Designer case study](https://a.wjerk.shop/case-study-libre-designer.html): why rented tools are a risk, not an inconvenience. Adobe cut off Venezuela's Creative Cloud access in 2019 to comply with US sanctions — paying the rent wasn't enough to guarantee access. "Libre," not "free" — the point was never to give design away for nothing, it's to not be locked into a tool, format, or ecosystem you can't get back out of.

Then the case that this isn't new or fringe: Stewart Brand's vernacular architecture, the GNU free software movement, David Bollier's and Elinor Ostrom's work on the commons as something people actively govern, not just a pile of resources. Ellen Lupton's argument that everyone deserves good typography, applied to the whole practice: if design is for everyone, the tools for making it need to be too.

Students pick a tool stack — Inkscape, GIMP, Blender, Scribus, Krita — and start building, using only openly licensed fonts and imagery.

---

## Day two — give it back

Students finish their piece. Before anything else, they license it — CC BY-SA, matching the terms of everything they built it from — and publish it somewhere public: a shared Are.na channel, a repo, wherever the class agrees. CC BY-SA builds a design bibliography as a side effect: you can trace where an idea came from and where it went next.

Close with an honest conversation about friction: lost credit, file-format headaches, unfamiliar tooling, a client who needs the file in InDesign. The goal was never full adoption — it's having more tools available, and noticing how much of "good design" is just the accumulated habits of a few programs built by the same handful of people in the 1980s.

## Who it's for

Undergraduate studio, any level. Graduate seminar. 12–20 students. Requires laptops capable of running the chosen tools.

## What they leave with

One finished piece, made entirely outside the Adobe ecosystem. Working knowledge of at least one F/LOSS design tool. A licensed, public contribution back to the commons they drew from.

## What you need

- Two full days
- Computer lab or students' own laptops, F/LOSS tools pre-installed or installable
- Internet access for sourcing fonts/images and publishing the result
- A shared publishing destination decided in advance (Are.na channel, repo, etc.)

## Fee

$2,000–$3,000 plus travel and materials. Single-day version available — lecture only, no build.
