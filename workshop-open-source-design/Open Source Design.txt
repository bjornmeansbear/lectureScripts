5. Critical Software » Critical Design? - 5 minutes
	- Stallman
	- super fast xerox story
	- feels that the 4 freedoms are ethical, and if you aren't making software this way you are infringing on your fellow human beings rights.
	- This is oppositional software — software offering an alternative to reigning techno-capitalism. 
		- to extend Dunne and Raby's theses — if designers can/should make designs that critique the status quo and offer alternatives, wouldn't making those critical designs with critical software be correct?
		- Also! if you are a socially conscious designer, the original underpinnings of F/LOS might mean that F/LOSS tools ethics match yours!
			- side note: Counter culture 60s/70s socially concious designers used some related methods like Papaneks Nomadic Furniture, or Enzo Maris autoprogettazione? — these are share and share a like designs meant to better the world around them.
	 - Devin/Calvin open engagement?

6. Okay! so those are some conceptual reasons: — 1 minute
	- share more, collaborate more, distribute more — designs can be better, faster, help more people, and potentially fill more nitches (because they can be customized for special use cases)
	- if you care deeply about similar things as stallman — not limiting other human's freedoms, etc. then you might want tools that believe the same things.
	- applied to projects outside of class, applied to some work we did for the library (using their own resources to make new things), and applied to this book we produced...

8. The next time you go looking for F/LOS design and quickly find fonts, icons, templates, stock images and illustrations, and frameworks ask yourself: Is this all that F/LOS design means? Is that really all that’s possible? F/LOS offers up a pragmatic approach based on reviving how we as humans previously created socio-cultural artifacts; and a critical approach intentionally positioning itself in opposition to mainstream moderno-techno-capitalism.
