## The list

- John Berger, *Ways of Seeing* (Penguin/BBC, 1972) — how ideology and context determine what an image is understood to mean. Close to the whole premise of this workshop, in one book.
- Jeffery Keedy, “Zombie Modernism. It Lives!,” *Emigre* No. 34 (1995) — modernism revived as a look without the ideology that produced it; the direct source for this workshop’s “ideological bankruptcy” question.
- Katherine McCoy — co-chaired the design department at Cranbrook Academy of Art, 1971–1995, where postmodern graphic design got explicitly worked through via critical and literary theory.
- Ed Fella — CalArts; deliberately vernacular, “ugly” postmodern form as a rejection of corporate modernist polish.
- *Emigre* magazine, founded by Rudy VanderLans and Zuzana Licko — the actual publication where these ideological design fights played out in print, including sharp exchanges with modernists like Massimo Vignelli.
- Massimo Vignelli — the opposing position, worth including for friction: that clean, neutral, modernist form isn’t ideological at all, it’s simply correct.
- Rick Poynor, *No More Rules: Graphic Design and Postmodernism* (Laurence King / Yale University Press, 2003) — a full survey of this argument.
- Ken Garland, *First Things First* (1964), and its update *First Things First 2000* (signed by 33 designers, published simultaneously in *Adbusters*, *Emigre*, and the AIGA *Journal of Graphic Design*) — design’s social responsibility as an explicit ideological position, not a neutral craft choice.
- Ellen Lupton and Andrew Blauvelt — both already influences elsewhere in this practice; both have also written directly on this design-history moment.
- Elliott Earls — already credited in the semiotic zine workshop; his expressive, performance-driven typography is a living example of form carrying an explicit personal ideology, not just a historical reference.
- Roland Barthes, *Mythologies* — the semiotics foundation already used in the semiotic zine workshop; worth naming here too as the throughline between the two.
- Christopher Alexander, *Notes on the Synthesis of Form* and *A Pattern Language* — already cited in Day One; exact editions/pages still need tracking down (see Notes to self).
