# The Sustainabilitist Principles — Source Material

*Pulled from Kristian's personal wiki (`bjornpaedia.wjerk.shop`) and the published case study at [a.wjerk.shop/case-study-sustainabilitist-principles.html](https://a.wjerk.shop/case-study-sustainabilitist-principles.html), 2026-08-07. Original manifesto written 2009, based on MFA thesis work.*

## The manifesto

> Environmentalism's goal was environmental sustainability. Post-environmentalism's goal must be true sustainability.

A new term for the followers of post-environmentalism: **Sustainabilitists**. Sustainabilitists take over where the environmentalists leave off, moving sustainability from the realm of the environment into all realms. The following principles move ideas and actions toward the truly sustainable — and in keeping with its own principles, the manifesto and its concepts stay in a constant state of flux.

Keep an open mind and don't blindly follow the principles. Don't worry about "sameness" or "correctness" — solutions will differ in each scenario and situation. That's not worse, or wrong.

## The twenty principles

**Agility** — Change must happen fast, but raw speed alone is not enough. The rapid growth of industrialism got us into this mess; quick, precise, and nimble actions now will get us out of it.

**Change** — Sustainabilitists do not believe in change for the sake of it. Change should only happen when it is necessary. Maybe it's a better idea, maybe a material has become scarce, maybe the feeling is wrong — just make sure it's anything other than "let's change it!"

**Constraint** — The realm of restrictions, stipulations, and specifications. Defined at the outset, limitations can guide a solution. Not to be confused with sacrifice (reactive and loss-based) — constraint results from analyzing available options.

**Cycles** — Outputs must become inputs; there is no such thing as waste. Waste from one process becomes raw material in another. Nature is cyclical, so sustainabilitist processes must be too.

**Diversity** — Embrace diversity: it makes life more interesting. Nature has no overarching solution. Stop looking for one-size-fits-all. The only universal shall be sustainability itself — people, cultures, and localities are all different, requiring different solutions.

**Durability** — A cathedral was built to withstand the rigors of the temporal; a water bottle was not — it should be designed to disappear. Every material and use decision is a choice between durable and ephemeral.

**Education** — For sustainabilitist ideals to be accepted, they must first be presented and understood by the public. Presenting the possibility of change brings us one step closer to inciting it. Easy, understandable ways are best — especially those that avoid condescension.

**Elegance** — Wealthy "elegance" abounds, but true elegance appears when simplicity and craft are used to solve everyday problems. Sustainabilitists desire ease and grace in all situations.

**Entropy** — The universe moves toward chaos. This is something to embrace. Design seeks order, and the order of the universe is controlled degradation.

**Ephemerality** — See Durability: the choice between durable and ephemeral is made at the design stage, deliberately, not by accident.

**Flexibility** — There is no perfect approach — the future will unfold in unpredictable ways. Allow for adaptive reuse and a willingness to change direction or tactics to fit circumstances.

**Knowledge** — Information wants to be free, and it's time we let it. Disseminating knowledge has never been easier. We need to increase, not limit, people's access. Sustainabilitists must become masters of knowledge.

**Meaning** — All great works that elicit a response have one thing in common: they mean something. Focus on intent, purpose, and substance — the context — along with form and function.

**Momentum** — We are currently starting from rest. Once underway, our processes and ideals will carry themselves into culture, manufacturing, and government by their own momentum. First, change the inertia of current operations.

**Quality** — Better, happier, more fulfilling: this is what quality means in sustainable design. The aim is to provide quality to everyone in elegant, elemental ways. Flimsy, inferior goods and services have no place in a sustainable future.

**Services** — We want the functions objects provide, not the objects themselves. Approach appliances and tools in terms of "services, not stuff" and "use, not own." Against intentional obsolescence, for reusability and repairability.

**Solutions** — (Text as recorded shares language with Quality in the source wiki — worth Kristian's eye on whether this is a duplication or an intentional echo before this goes anywhere public.)

**Systems** — Current systems — mass consumption yielding mass waste — are out of date and faulty. It's pointless to resolve designs within these systems. Creating and exploiting new, better systems is the main directive for sustainable design.

**Time** — Allowing time to pass opens the door to adaptability and evolution. Adopt the gradual change of nature into design processes. Time allows for keeping and improving the good while discarding the bad.

**Wealth** — Sustainabilitist "wealth" encompasses all areas of resources — knowledge, information, water, food — not just the monetary. The sustainabilitist world revolves around the fair distribution and dissemination of these values.

## BauErden — Build Earth

> The ultimate aim of design must be restoring harmony in the carbon cycle. No longer can we produce meaningless prints, tchotchkes, devices, apps, objects, and software. No design may be created without first asking "does this need to exist?" and/or "does this help draw down carbon from the atmosphere?" and/or "does this re-balance natural systems?"

We need the conscious, cooperative effort of all artists, designers, politicians, craftspeople, scientists, farmers. New design education must aid in this — a new pedagogical approach focused on the liminal, on designers as connectors.

Designers must recognize anew the composite character of "climate" — as a holistic entity and in its separate, interconnected parts. Only then will designing be imbued with the spirit it has lost as mere servant of capitalism. Old styles and models for designing are not capable of producing the required new unity.

We must merge design with the workshop, the science lab, the forest, the internet, the ocean depths, the lecture hall, the meadow, the studio, and the public square. Designers must be the nexus of connection between all interests and needs. Our client is our climate. Our goals: drawing down carbon, the welfare of all life, and the restoration of spaceship earth.

## On whether sustainable design has a "look" (from "Thoughts on Sustainabilitist Aesthetics")

A recurring question, unresolved by design: is there a particular aesthetic, visual trope, or look that "sustainable graphic design" is supposed to have? Working through it against John Ehrenfeld's claim in *Flourishing* — "the key to doing something about sustainability is that you first have to say what you want to sustain" — and Alain de Botton's *The Architecture of Happiness*.

A concrete case: a guest furniture-maker presented work built entirely from reclaimed materials. Students found it aesthetically undesirable — the found materials "spoke too loudly," everything read as visibly Reused, in a way that felt unrefined rather than considered. It became a recurring tension in their own projects: work that wears sustainability on its sleeve versus work that just tries to be good design that also happens to be sustainable.

The conclusion reached: there may be no single correct aesthetic. What the strongest student projects shared wasn't a style or material — it was a message. "Maybe it is just the message that matters."
