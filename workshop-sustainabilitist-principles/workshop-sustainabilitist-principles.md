# THE SUSTAINABILITIST PRINCIPLES

*A workshop on building your own sustainability manifesto, as an object, not a poster*

> Working title, matching the existing project name. Alternates at bottom.

---

## The pitch

In 2009, working through a crisis of "what does sustainable design actually look like," the answer turned out not to be another poster: found paperback books, screen-printed with definitions, connected by embroidery floss cut to the longest reusable length. A manifesto you could hang on a wall, built entirely from what was already at hand.

Students don't read a manifesto about the future of design. They build one — a physical, modular object made from found materials, embodying a principle they choose or write themselves, joining a growing collective installation by the end of the workshop.

---

## Day one — the twenty principles

Short lecture: the Sustainabilitist Manifesto and its twenty principles (Agility, Constraint, Cycles, Durability, and so on), BauErden's harder question — does this design need to exist, does it draw down carbon, does it re-balance natural systems — and the open, unresolved question of whether sustainable design even has a "look."

Each student picks a principle (existing or their own) and starts gathering material: found books, objects, whatever's already around, no new purchases.

## Day two — build the object

Students embody their principle physically — printed, written, or otherwise fixed onto found material — and connect their piece into a shared installation with the rest of the group, the same way the original piece linked books together with thread.

Close with a critique grounded in "Thoughts on Sustainabilitist Aesthetics": did the group end up with one aesthetic, or many? Does that matter? What's the actual message everyone's object shares, if the materials and styles don't?

---

## Who it's for

Undergraduate studio, any level. Graduate seminar. 12–20 students.

## What they leave with

A finished, physical piece of a shared manifesto. A working answer to whether "sustainable" has a required look, tested against their own object instead of argued about in the abstract. A working understanding of the twenty principles as a design lens, not a checklist.

## What you need

- Two full days
- Studio space with wall or floor room for the growing collective installation
- Found materials: books, thread, whatever the group can gather — the point is to avoid new material purchases
- Basic print or mark-making tools (screen printing if available, but pen and paper work)

## Fee

$2,000–$3,000 plus travel and materials. Single-day version available — lecture and small individual pieces, no collective installation.
